> Working draft for the Amadeus Open Source Board discussion on 11 September 2026. The figures below are dated captures, not a live dashboard.

Amadeus open source has a story that started before this report: early experimentation, ecosystem participation, the 2023 Manifesto, the 2025 Open Source Community and Open Source Board, and a 2026 opportunity to make the work easier to understand from outside the company.

## What the current evidence says

### The Board is a living pipeline

The authenticated Open Source Board exports captured on 10 September 2026 contain **39 cases** created in Q1 through Q3-to-date:

- 10 cases in Q1;
- 21 cases in Q2; and
- 8 cases from 1 July through 10 September in Q3.

The Board workflow records more than a binary decision. The captured statuses include `Approved`, `Published`, `Working on contribution`, `In Progress`, `Todo` and `Unknown`. They are different from GitHub’s `Open` and `Closed` issue state.

The cases span upstream observability fixes, AI engineering primitives, security research, developer tools, testing, cloud infrastructure, data engineering, sustainability and public project proposals. The discussions show the mechanism at work: reviewers ask for upstream issues, check project health and alternatives, improve scope, protect internal context and guide contributors toward a useful public contribution.

### The contributor cohort is active and broad

A refresh of the current `AmadeusITGroup/os_contributors` team captured **180 members** and analyzed GitHub activity from 2024 onward:

- 5,692 external contributions;
- 4,339 external pull requests;
- 1,353 external issues;
- 3,821 merged pull requests;
- 469 external projects; and
- 242 projects with contributions from more than one team member.

The analyzer reports an 88.1% team pull-request merge rate and a 51.6% collaboration rate. These are useful signals of activity and integration, not a complete measure of open source value. GitHub does not capture every form of mentoring, documentation, speaking, governance or community care.

The cohort is also not the same population as the draft report’s approximately 220 active community members across 12 countries. Both figures can be useful, but they must retain their labels and methods.

## What the project stories add

Numbers show motion. Project stories show what that motion means.

- **Perses** represents an Amadeus-initiated observability project growing through a broader ecosystem.
- **Software Water Impact Resources** shows how research can become a reproducible public resource with source provenance, limitations and explicit licensing.
- **Yggy** shows the value of publishing a useful scope boundary: its public documentation explains the problem, the intended architecture and what the shipped v1 actually contains.
- **Think Inside the Box** shows a different pathway: a community RFC that shares a reference implementation and a proposed standard while openly naming security, governance and adoption questions.

These are not interchangeable success stories. They represent different pathways: lead and grow, share and transfer, scope honestly, and propose and invite.

## A public report should make the method visible

The framework proposed for the next public version is:

1. **Journey and principles** — why open source matters and how the Manifesto guides participation.
2. **Scope and method** — what repositories, teams, dates and statuses are counted.
3. **Portfolio** — non-fork projects as the primary project-story scope; forks as possible contribution signals.
4. **Participation** — maintainers, reviewers, issue reporters, researchers, speakers, documentation authors and community organisers alongside code contributors.
5. **Evidence and learning** — project stories that include context, action, governance, outcome, limitations and next step.
6. **Invitation** — a clear way to contribute, ask a question or share a new story.

This is deliberately closer to a living evidence system than a yearly vanity report. It should be possible to rerun the captures, inspect the raw evidence and understand where a claim came from.

## What is still pending

The supplied draft report and key-ideas document contain valuable working figures, including mapped contribution hotspots and a broader community baseline. Those figures should be refreshed and assigned an owner before they are presented as current public metrics.

The public story should also avoid a few easy mistakes:

- an `Approved` Board item is not automatically `Published`;
- a fork is not automatically an Amadeus-led project;
- a project’s target architecture is not automatically its shipped scope; and
- a high contribution count is not the same thing as community health.

## The next step

Make the story shareable without making it less honest: approve a small set of public project cards, agree the metric definitions and refresh cadence, and invite the wider community to help improve the record.
